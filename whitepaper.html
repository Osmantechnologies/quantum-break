The Honest Q-Day Meter

A public, verifiable measurement of how hard the maths behind Bitcoin and Ethereum signatures really is, run live in your browser.

01The question

In October 2026 a well-known Ethereum researcher argued that AI-driven mathematics might weaken elliptic-curve signatures before quantum computers do, and urged holders to move funds to addresses that have never signed. Experts disagreed loudly. Nobody could settle it by arguing.

This project does not predict anything. It measures: how big a key the best known attack can recover, how fast, and how far that is from the 256-bit keys that protect real wallets.

02How a run works

Each run makes a fresh random key of a chosen size, shows its public half first, then searches for the private half using Pollard's kangaroo method: two groups of walkers hop along the number line, one from known positions, one from the public key, until they land on the same spot. The distance between their starts is the answer.

When the answer is found, the page multiplies it back and checks it matches the public key. Anyone can repeat that check. Every key is made by the page seconds before; no real wallet is ever touched.

Every extra 2 bits doubles the work. The gap from today's browser runs to a real Bitcoin key is roughly 2100 times. The meter exists to make that gap visible.

04The flies

The 3D sandbox shows the walkers as two swarms of flies: pale flies start from known positions, dark flies start from the public key. Their positions on screen come straight from the running search. When two flies meet, a light pillar marks the collision.

The Room is the flies' home: a hall of rigs and racks. Its hive panel tells a story of the colony, a Queen, Foremen and Workers, and is labelled as simulation. When a real search is running on the meter, its real numbers appear in the same log, marked as real.

05What this does not do

It does not show that AI will or will not find a new shortcut. It measures the attack we have today. It never targets anyone's wallet, and it does not hunt the Bitcoin puzzle prizes; those appear only as a public scoreboard.
